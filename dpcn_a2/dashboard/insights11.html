🔬 Scientific Interpretation & Network Physics Analysis

Plotting all four topologies simultaneously reveals two stark structural phase divides in the CSIR Chemistry cluster. First, Full Co-Authorship (blue solid) reaches 50% adoption fastest (T50 = 6.60 steps, saturating at 97.0%) because the ~7,200 junior researchers (PhD scholars and postdocs) serve as dense, unstratified transmission conduits bridging otherwise isolated PI laboratories. Thus, scientific knowledge diffuses cyclically across permanent authors, but remains locally bounded along historical paper DAGs.

🔬 Scientific Interpretation & Institutional Asymmetry

The seed-origin simulation reveals a critical core-periphery asymmetry in the CSIR cluster. When an innovation originates in the large urban laboratories—CSIR-IICT (Hyderabad) or CSIR-NCL (Pune)—it diffuses with comparable, rapid early velocity (I(3) \approx 4.7\%\text{--}5.1\%) and reaches ~70% of all CSIR cadre faculty. Both IICT and NCL possess large rosters (129 and 151 active scientists) with extensive internal multidisciplinary divisions (catalysis, organic synthesis, process chemistry) providing immediate local critical mass. In stark contrast, when a discovery originates at CSIR-CECRI (Karaikudi) under the Cadre-only network, diffusion experiences severe structural stagnation: velocity at step 3 is only 1.54%, and the cascade saturates at a meager 47.57% final reach. Because CECRI is geographically distant in Tamil Nadu with a smaller cadre cohort (26 active faculty) focused on electrochemistry, its faculty lack direct collaborative bridges into Pune and Hyderabad. However, when junior scholars are reintroduced (Full Authorship), CECRI's reach surges to 91.01% (T50 = 9.0 steps), demonstrating that CECRI's scientific impact depends almost entirely on mobile PhD scholars co-publishing with partner labs.

🔬 Scientific Interpretation & Jensen's Inequality in Network Dynamics

Comparing the deterministic Discrete-Time Markov Chain against the stochastic Monte Carlo average provides empirical confirmation of Jensen's Inequality in networked contagion processes. NIMFA operates under the individual-based mean-field assumption that neighbor infection states are statistically independent (E[x_i x_j] = E[x_i]E[x_j]). However, real co-authorship networks feature dense, highly localized collaborative cliques where co-authors' infection states are strongly positively correlated. Because the discrete non-infection transmission function f(x) = \prod (1 - \beta x_j) is concave with respect to multiple infected neighbors, Jensen's inequality proves that the mean-field approximation acts as a strict theoretical upper bound on empirical spreading (I_{\text{NIMFA}}(t) \ge I_{\text{MC}}(t)). Across our simulations, this produces a consistent positive gap of \Delta(t) \in [+1.8\%, +5.8\%] and accelerates the predicted time to 50% adoption (T_{50}^{\text{NIMFA}} \le T_{50}^{\text{MC}}).

Mathematical Formulation: Discrete-Time NIMFA

Let xi(t) ∈ [0, 1] represent the exact probability that researcher i has adopted the method at step t. Under independent neighbor transmission, the individual-based Markov state transition is:

xi(t+1) = xi(t) + (1 - xi(t)) × [ 1 - ∏j ∈ Nin(i) (1 - βji × xj(t)) ]
Exact Dynamical Sensitivity:
The marginal influence of researcher i is computed as the derivative:
Φi(t) = ∂ ∑k=1N xk(t) / ∂ xi(0)
This quantifies the exact expected number of additional researchers across the entire CSIR cluster who adopt the innovation at time t solely due to researcher i's initial origination.

Why Data Cleaning Was Necessary

When scraping CSIR staff directories (particularly CSIR-IICT), technical staff such as Senior Technical Officers, Technical Assistants, and Technicians were listed under departmental rosters. The raw scraper inadvertently captured their job titles into the author name field (e.g. "Dr. X Sr. Technical Officer (3)") while recording their designation generically as "Scientist".

Rule-Based Filtering Applied:
Any roster record matching the regular expression:
/(?:Sr|Senior|Principal)?\s*(?:Technical Officer|Technical Assistant|Technician)(?:\s*\(\d+\))?$/i
was stripped of job titles, excluded from faculty_master.csv, and cataloged in data/review/roster_excluded.csv.
Manual Deduplication & Disambiguation
  • Cross-Institute Duplicate Removal: Dr. D. Srinivasa Reddy was listed simultaneously on the NCL Organic Chemistry page and as Director of CSIR-IICT. Kept uniquely as IICT Director (FAC_IICT_0315) and stale NCL listing removed.
  • OpenAlex Catch-All Profile Disambiguation: Profile A5087530076 had merged multiple distinct scientists into an ambiguous "A. V." record. These profiles were un-linked and assigned to paper-printed names.
  • Paper Row Reclassification: 282 dataset rows attributable to technical staff were retained as affiliated_author rather than faculty rows, ensuring complete publication recall while protecting faculty metric integrity.
🔬 Scientific Interpretation & Data Integrity Rationale

Rigorous data cleaning is the cornerstone of trustworthy bibliometrics. Had the 119 technical officers and technicians been erroneously retained in the scientific cadre, the CSIR Chemistry cluster's median h-index would have been artificially deflated from 7.0 down to 6.0, and 1,290 co-authorship ties would have carried distorted job title suffixes. By segregating permanent scientific investigators from support cadre and contractual scholars, we ensure that faculty performance rankings, succession risk analyses, and topological diffusion simulations accurately reflect the institutional governance of CSIR laboratories.
