How does a new idea spread through CSIR's chemistry labs?

We let a new method or reagent "infect" the labs' collaboration and citation networks and watch how far and how fast it travels: through everyone who publishes, through faculty alone, along citations, by field and by lab of origin. Every number below is computed from our data, every formula is written out, and every claim is checked automatically.

02 / 08

The model, written out

How exactly is the spreading of an idea simulated?

A Susceptible–Infected (SI) process in discrete steps: once a researcher adopts the idea they keep it and keep passing it to collaborators. One step is one round of contact, not a calendar unit.

1 · Stochastic SI (Monte Carlo)

Each step, every adopter \(u\) tries once to pass the idea to each non-adopting neighbour \(v\), succeeding with probability \(\beta_{uv}\):

\[ P\big(v \text{ adopts at } t+1 \,\big|\, \text{state at } t\big) = 1-\prod_{u \in \mathcal{N}(v),\ u \text{ adopted}} \big(1-\beta_{uv}\big). \]

We start from one seed, repeat with independent random numbers, and average the share of adopters \(I(t)\).

2 · Mean field (NIMFA)

Instead of sampling, track each person's adoption probability \(x_i(t)\) and assume neighbours are independent:

\[ x_i(t+1) = x_i(t) + \big(1-x_i(t)\big)\Big[1-\prod_{j \to i}\big(1-\beta_{ji}\,x_j(t)\big)\Big],\qquad x_{\text{seed}}(0)=1. \]

Neighbours' states are in fact positively correlated (if your co-author has the idea, their other co-authors probably do too), so mean field is expected to over-estimate spreading. We do not assume this: section 05 measures the gap.

3 · Speed: T50

\(T_{50}\) = first step at which at least half of the target group has adopted. Some runs never get there within 15 steps, so we report the censored median: the median counting "not reached" as later than any number, and "not reached" if the median run never gets there.

4 · How likely is a hand-over? \(\beta_{uv}\)

People share ideas more readily with collaborators who work on similar topics. Each person (or paper) has a profile \(\mathbf{p}\): their paper counts per OpenAlex subfield, scaled to length 1. Similarity \(\cos(u,v)=\mathbf{p}_u\cdot\mathbf{p}_v\).

Field study (section 04), with \(s_v(F)\) = share of \(v\)'s papers in field \(F\):

\[ \text{A (topic-weighted, whole network):}\quad \beta_{uv}=\beta_0\,\cos(u,v)\,\max\!\big(s_v(F),\,\varepsilon\big) \] \[ \text{B (field only):}\quad \beta_{uv}=\beta_0 \ \text{ inside the field's own network} \]

Network comparison (sections 05–07): \(\beta_{uv}=\beta_0\,\max(0.1,\cos(u,v))\); for a paper the profile is its own subfield, so \(\cos=1\) for the same subfield and \(0\) otherwise.

5 · Thresholds

With recovery (an adopter stops passing the idea on), spreading only takes off above a threshold. Standard predictions:

\[ \beta_c^{\text{HMF}}=\frac{\langle k\rangle}{\langle k^2\rangle},\qquad \beta_c^{\text{QMF}}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\max}(A)},\qquad T_c^{\text{SIR}}=\frac{\langle k\rangle}{\langle k^2\rangle-\langle k\rangle}. \]

Plain SI has no threshold: any \(\beta>0\) eventually reaches the whole connected network. Section 03 tests the SIR threshold directly.

03 / 08

Is there a tipping point? Theory vs. simulation

Is there a point below which an idea dies out, and does the textbook formula find it?

If each person passes the idea on only once (with probability \(T\)) and then stops, the outbreak from a seed is exactly the seed's cluster when every collaboration link is kept with probability \(T\) (bond percolation). So we can measure the tipping point directly and compare it with the formula \(T_c=\langle k\rangle/(\langle k^2\rangle-\langle k\rangle)\).

Outbreak size vs. transmissibility

\(S(T)\) = share of the network in the largest outbreak; \(\chi(T)=\sum_{\text{other clusters}} s^2/N\) = mean size of a small outbreak, which peaks at the tipping point. random repeats per \(T\), \(T\) in steps of 0.005.
