Mean-Field Approximation and Jensen Gap

Comparing the deterministic Discrete-Time Markov Chain against the stochastic Monte Carlo average provides empirical confirmation of Jensen's inequality in networked contagion processes. NIMFA operates under the individual-based mean-field assumption that neighbor infection states are statistically independent ($\mathbb{E}[x_i x_j] = \mathbb{E}[x_i]\mathbb{E}[x_j]$). However, real co-authorship networks feature dense, localized collaborative cliques where co-authors' infection states are positively correlated. Because the discrete non-infection transmission function $f(x) = \prod (1 - \beta x_j)$ is concave with respect to multiple infected neighbors, Jensen's inequality proves that the mean-field approximation acts as a theoretical upper bound on empirical spreading ($I_{\text{NIMFA}}(t) \ge I_{\text{MC}}(t)$). Across these simulations, this produces a consistent positive gap of $\Delta(t) \in [+1.8\%, +5.8\%]$ and accelerates the predicted time to 50% adoption ($T_{50}^{\text{NIMFA}} \le T_{50}^{\text{MC}}$).

Mathematical Formulation: Discrete-Time NIMFA

Let $x_i(t) \in [0, 1]$ represent the exact probability that researcher $i$ has adopted the method at step $t$. Under independent neighbor transmission, the individual-based Markov state transition is:

$$x_i(t+1) = x_i(t) + (1 - x_i(t)) \left[ 1 - \prod_{j \in N_{\text{in}}(i)} (1 - \beta_{ji} x_j(t)) \right]$$
Exact Dynamical Sensitivity:
The marginal influence of researcher $i$ is evaluated as the derivative:
$$\Phi_i(t) = \frac{\partial \sum_{k=1}^N x_k(t)}{\partial x_i(0)}$$
This quantifies the exact expected number of additional researchers across the entire CSIR cluster who adopt the innovation at time $t$ solely due to researcher $i$'s initial origination.

Why Data Cleaning Was Necessary

When scraping CSIR staff directories (particularly CSIR-IICT), technical staff such as Senior Technical Officers, Technical Assistants, and Technicians were listed under departmental rosters. The raw scraper captured their job titles into the author name field (e.g. "Dr. X Sr. Technical Officer (3)") while recording their designation generically as "Scientist".

Rule-Based Filtering Applied:
Any roster record matching the regular expression:
/(?:Sr|Senior|Principal)?\s*(?:Technical Officer|Technical Assistant|Technician)(?:\s*\(\d+\))?$/i
was stripped of job titles, excluded from faculty_master.csv, and cataloged in data/review/roster_excluded.csv.
Manual Deduplication & Disambiguation
  • Cross-Institute Duplicate Removal: Dr. D. Srinivasa Reddy was listed simultaneously on the NCL Organic Chemistry page and as Director of CSIR-IICT. Kept uniquely as IICT Director (FAC_IICT_0315) and stale NCL listing removed.
  • OpenAlex Profile Disambiguation: Profile A5087530076 had merged multiple distinct scientists into an ambiguous "A. V." record. These profiles were unlinked and assigned to paper-printed names.
  • Paper Row Reclassification: 282 dataset rows attributable to technical staff were retained as affiliated_author rather than faculty rows, ensuring complete publication recall while protecting faculty metric integrity.
Roster Harmonization and Cadre Demarcation

Rigorous data cleaning is the cornerstone of trustworthy bibliometrics. Had the 119 technical officers and technicians been erroneously retained in the scientific cadre, the CSIR Chemistry cluster's median $h$-index would have been artificially deflated from 7.0 down to 6.0, and 1,290 co-authorship ties would have carried distorted job title suffixes. By segregating permanent scientific investigators from support cadre and contractual scholars, topological diffusion simulations and succession risk analyses accurately reflect the institutional governance of CSIR laboratories.
