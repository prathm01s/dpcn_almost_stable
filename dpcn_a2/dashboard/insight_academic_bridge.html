The Academic-Industrial Bridge: Idea Exporter or Idea Consumer?

Through analysis of 192,994 verified directed citation links across India's higher education ecosystem, the directional Knowledge Exchange Ratio ($K_{ER}$) evaluates whether CSIR chemistry labs function primarily as net knowledge suppliers or fundamental theory consumers relative to Tier-1 Flagships (IITs, IISc) and Tier-2 Regional State Universities.

Primary Data Sources

  • Citation Network: data/final/citation_network.csv containing 192,994 directed links with source DOI, target DOI, source institute, target institute, and publication year.
  • Indian Institutional Connections: data/final/indian_connections.csv recording joint papers and shared citations across 150+ Indian universities.
  • Institutional Categorization: Grouped into Tier-1 (Legacy IITs + IISc) and Tier-2 (Regional State/Central Universities).

Mathematical Formulations

$$K_{ER}(i, j) = \frac{C_{j \to i}}{C_{i \to j}} = \frac{\text{Citations from HEI } j \text{ to CSIR } i}{\text{Citations from CSIR } i \text{ to HEI } j}$$

Knowledge Exchange Ratio ($K_{ER}$): Values $> 1.0$ indicate Net Knowledge Exporters (CSIR feeds academic research). Values $< 1.0$ indicate Net Knowledge Importers.

$$\Delta C(i, j) = C_{j \to i} - C_{i \to j}$$

Preprocessing & Exclusion Rules

  • Self-Citations Excluded: Internal cross-citations within the same lab are isolated to evaluate genuine inter-institutional diffusion.
  • Affiliation Disambiguation: Standardized OpenAlex institution strings for IITs, IISc, and state universities to prevent split-campus false negatives.

Scientific & Bibliometric Interpretation

The Knowledge Exchange Ratio reveals a clear structural division in Indian chemical science. CSIR acts as a net knowledge exporter to technical universities that focus on engineering translation—most notably IIT Hyderabad ($K_{ER} = 2.031$), Alagappa University ($K_{ER} = 1.581$), and IIT Roorkee ($K_{ER} = 1.046$). In these relationships, university researchers cite CSIR's chemical synthesis and catalytic materials papers significantly more than CSIR cites them back. Conversely, CSIR acts as a net knowledge consumer of elite basic-science institutions—most prominently IISc Bangalore ($K_{ER} = 0.410$) and University of Hyderabad ($K_{ER} = 0.389$). CSIR chemists reference IISc's theoretical and physical chemistry models to inform applied synthesis pipelines.

Exchange Dynamics

Plotting incoming citations ($C_{\text{received}}$) against outgoing references ($C_{\text{sent}}$) highlights institutional symmetry. Institutions near the 45-degree parity diagonal (such as IIT Guwahati, IIT Delhi, and IIT Kanpur) maintain balanced scientific exchanges with CSIR, with citation exchanges hovering around 1,000–1,200 citations in each direction. IISc Bangalore stands out as a high-volume outlier with over 3,100 citations received from CSIR, reflecting its role as the national baseline for fundamental chemical physics.
