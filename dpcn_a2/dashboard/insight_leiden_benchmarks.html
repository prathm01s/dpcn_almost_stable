Global Leiden "Crown Indicator" Benchmarking: Beyond Raw Citation Counts

Raw citation metrics are confounded by publication year, disciplinary citation customs, and paper volume. To determine the global scientific standing of Indian CSIR chemistry, the methodology of the CWTS Leiden Ranking Open Edition is applied. Using field-and-year normalization on the 14,352-paper corpus, this study calculates the proportion of papers in the global top 10% ($PP_{\text{top 10\%}}$), the top 1% ($PP_{\text{top 1\%}}$), and the Mean Normalized Citation Score ($MNCS$) against the Max Planck Society, Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), CNRS, UC Berkeley, and peer Indian institutions.

Primary Data Sources

  • Corpus Normalization: 14,352 papers from data/final/group24_data_extended.csv evaluated across OpenAlex subfields and publication years (2016–2026).
  • CWTS Leiden Ranking Open Edition: Official 2023/2024 Physical Sciences and Engineering / Chemistry benchmark data from the Centre for Science and Technology Studies (CWTS), Leiden University (open.leidenranking.com).

Mathematical Formulations

$$PP_{\text{top 10\%}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=1}^N \mathbb{I}(\text{Percentile}_{f, y}(k) \ge 90.0) \times 100\%$$

Percentage of papers exceeding the 90th percentile of citations in their specific subfield $f$ and year $y$. Global expected value = 10.0%.

$$MNCS = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=1}^N \frac{C_k}{\mu_{f(k), y(k)}}$$

Mean Normalized Citation Score: Ratio of observed citations to world subfield-year average. $MNCS = 1.0$ is exact world average; $>1.0$ is above world average.

Normalization & Edge Rules

  • Zero Imputation Protection: For subfield-year cohorts where world average citations $\mu = 0$, baseline is set to 1.0 to prevent division by zero.
  • OpenAlex Full Counting: Following CWTS Open Edition conventions, full counting of institute publications is applied.

Scientometric Interpretation

When normalized for subfield and publication year, CSIR chemistry ranks as an active global research cluster. The consolidated cluster achieves a $PP_{\text{top 10\%}}$ of 14.42%—meaning that CSIR produces 44% more top-decile chemistry papers than would be expected by baseline distribution. This performance exceeds top Indian academic universities (IISc at 9.8% and IIT Bombay at 9.1%), surpasses major European national centers like France's CNRS (12.6%), and closely matches the Chinese Academy of Sciences (13.8%). While leading institutions like UC Berkeley (22.8%) and Germany's Max Planck Society (20.4%) maintain higher proportions, CSIR sustains strong global representation within its operational budget.

Disciplinary Strengths

Examining the Crown Indicators across specific chemistry disciplines reveals that CSIR's highest concentration of top publications occurs in Renewable Energy & Electrochemistry ($PP_{\text{top 10\%}} = 15.8\%$, driven by CECRI and NCL battery/hydrogen research) and Catalysis & Materials Chemistry ($PP_{\text{top 10\%}} = 15.2\%$). Even in classical Organic Synthesis, where global citation competition is intense, CSIR maintains an above-average top-10% rate of 14.1%, supported by IICT's heterocyclic and chiral synthesis methodologies.
